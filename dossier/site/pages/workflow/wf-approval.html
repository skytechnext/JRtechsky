Blueprint Approval — three decision-makers sign before anything is built

No sheet of stainless is cut and no first-fix point is drilled until the Kitchen Blueprint Pack carries three approvals: the restaurant owner, the head chef, and JR-Tech's staff together with the appointed contractors. Each signs for what only they can judge — the commercial decision, the cooking workflow, and buildability — and the fully signed revision becomes the construction issue that releases fabrication. Worked example continues with Restoran Selat 88, 120 seats, 82 m² kitchen.

1 · Who signs — and what each signature means

An owner can sign for the money. But a kitchen the head chef cannot run gets re-drawn after opening at ten times the cost, and a layout the contractor cannot set out turns into hacked tiles and stale drawings. Three signatures move those arguments onto paper, where a revision costs 48 hours instead of a closure week.

Approver 1

Restaurant owner

Signs for the commercial decision.

  • Total budget, and the CAPEX-versus-rental split on each schedule line
  • Seating capacity and service style the layout actually delivers
  • Brand and guest experience — open kitchen, show elements, dining-room sightlines
  • Target opening date against the programme
  • Licence obligations and owner actions on the compliance checklist
Signature means: "Build this kitchen, at this budget, for this opening date." Final say on budget, brand and scope. Cannot override a safety or statutory item flagged by JR-Tech.
Approver 2

Head chef

Signs for the cooking workflow — the person who will run the line at 216 dishes an hour.

  • Station sequence and one-way flow against the real menu: mise → fire → pass
  • Reach, clearance and working heights at every station; pass and expedite sightlines
  • Equipment class per dish — wok, steamer, fryer, grill, induction — and burner count at peak
  • Cold, dry and live storage volumes against par levels and delivery days
  • Dishwash loop, hand-wash and prep-sink positions for how the brigade actually moves
Signature means: "My brigade can cook this menu on this line at full house." Final say on station workflow and equipment mix within the approved budget. If no head chef is appointed yet, the owner names a chef designate at the first meeting; JR-Tech's consulting chef reviews as stand-in and the appointed head chef re-confirms before construction issue.
Approver 3

JR-Tech staff & contractors

Sign for buildability, safety and compliance — JR-Tech's design engineer and M&E supervisor, with the owner's appointed renovation, M&E and licensed gas contractors.

  • Clearances, hood capture and make-up air, connected loads and diversity, drainage falls
  • Point schedule matches the site survey — DB capacity, supply upgrade, cable and pipe routes
  • Fabrication feasibility — panel sizes through the delivery route, site assembly, finishes
  • Setting-out on the real slab; floor falls, grease-trap position, LPG cage and interlock
  • MBPP, BOMBA, DOSH and Suruhanjaya Tenaga requirements met on the drawings
Signature means: "First-fix and fabrication can proceed from this drawing set without site improvisation." Final say on safety, statutory compliance and buildability — a JR-Tech safety veto is never overridden by budget or preference.
The rule of the gate: all three, every revision. A pack with two signatures is a draft. Partial approvals are not accepted either — the equipment schedule is not frozen while the layout is still open, because a moved fryer changes the hood length, the gas run and the budget together.

3 · The approval sequence — parallel reviews, one walkthrough, one markup set

  1. Day 0 — Rev A issued. PDF, DWG and XLSX land in the shared Odoo project; each approver receives a portal invitation and a review checklist written for their lens.
  2. Days 1–3 — Parallel reviews. The owner reviews with the Financial Pack open. The head chef walks the plan station by station — taped out on the actual floor where the premises is accessible. JR-Tech runs the engineering check; the contractors confirm setting-out, DB capacity and drainage runs against the site they will build on.
  3. Day 4 — Joint walkthrough, 60 minutes. All three parties in one room or one call, producing one consolidated markup set. No parallel WhatsApp threads. Disagreements are settled by the decision rules in section 4 and recorded with the markup.
  4. Within 48 hours — Rev B. The engine re-flows layout, schedules, loads and budget from the single model; the change log lists every markup and how it was resolved.
  5. Signatures. Owner, head chef, and JR-Tech with the contractors sign Rev B in Odoo Sign. The signed revision is the construction issue; fabrication work orders and the first-fix checklist release the same day. A second markup round, if needed, repeats steps 3–5 and is included in the pack price.

4 · Decision rules when approvers disagree

Three approvers will not always agree — that is the point of having three. The rules below decide quickly and leave a record, so the walkthrough ends with a markup set, not a stalemate.

Typical conflictWho decidesHow it is recorded
Head chef wants a second steamer; owner wants the budget heldOwner decides on moneyChef's request logged as Alternative B with its cost delta; the spare point stays on the drawing so it can be added later
Head chef wants the fryer beside the pass; JR-Tech says the hood run and gas run become non-compliantJR-Tech on safety or code; owner if it is only costLoad and hood consequence written against the markup; compliant alternative offered in Rev B
Contractor reports the drainage fall cannot be achieved in the existing slabContractor's site fact standsJR-Tech re-flows drainage and grease-trap position; owner informed of any cost change before signing
Owner wants an open kitchen; head chef is concerned about heat and noise on the lineOwner decides brandJR-Tech prices the glass screen and hood options for the choice; chef's mitigation noted in the Operations Pack
Any MBPP, BOMBA, DOSH or Suruhanjaya Tenaga requirementNon-negotiableNobody signs a set that fails the compliance checklist; the item is fixed in Rev B

Three principles behind the rules

  1. Sign for your lens. Each party signs for what they can judge — the owner for the commercial decision, the head chef for the cooking workflow, JR-Tech and the contractors for what can be built, certified and serviced.
  2. One markup set, one revision. Comments are consolidated at the walkthrough, so the engine re-flows once and the change log is readable.
  3. The signed set is frozen. A change after construction issue is a new revision through the same three-party gate, priced transparently — never a verbal instruction on site.

Illustrative: moving the twin-tank fryer (E11) 1.2 m along Selat 88's hot line. At the review stage it is a re-generation inside the 48-hour re-issue; after opening it is two closure days at a normal-day revenue of about RM 14k plus the works.

5 · How the gate runs in Odoo

StepOdoo mechanism
Approver registerContacts on the CRM lead tagged by role — Owner, Head chef, JR-Tech design lead, JR-Tech M&E supervisor, Contractor (renovation / M&E / LPG). Captured at the first meeting; the engine will not issue Rev A without all three groups named.
Review tasksIssuing Rev A creates one review task per approver in the shared project, each with its lens-specific checklist and a due date that sets the 5-day clock.
MarkupsLogged as tasks on the revision, consolidated at the walkthrough; each carries its decision-rule outcome.
ApprovalAn Approvals request "Blueprint Rev x — construction issue" requiring every approver group; one rejection re-opens the revision.
SignaturesOdoo Sign template with four signature roles — owner, head chef, JR-Tech, contractors — timestamped on completion.
Release gateAn automated action keeps manufacturing orders and the Field Service first-fix checklist blocked until the Approvals record is approved and the Sign request is complete. The signed DWG/PDF set is locked in Documents; superseded revisions are archived, never deleted.

So what for the Odoo build

The approval gate is three standard apps wired together — Approvals for the multi-party decision, Sign for the construction-issue signatures, and a Project automation that holds manufacturing orders and the first-fix checklist until both are complete. It costs nothing in bespoke development and removes the most expensive failure in kitchen fit-out: building from a drawing somebody never agreed to. Scoped in the Requirements (BRD); the fabrication and installation flow it releases is in Department Workflows.
