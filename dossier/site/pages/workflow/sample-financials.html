Kitchen throughput — dinner-peak demand vs station capacity

At 2.6 dishes per cover (sharing-style Nyonya service), the 19:00–20:15 dinner peak asks roughly 110 dishes/hour of a 55 m² kitchen. Slow-simmer anchors (Perut Ikan, Gulai Tumis, Chap Chye) are batch-held from afternoon prep, so the à-la-minute load concentrates on the wok and fry stations.

Dinner carries the day — by design

Dinner delivers 59% of a normal day's revenue on 58% of the covers. The kitchen is engineered around the 19:00–20:15 peak; the wok line is deliberately the constraint at 85%, because rempah-heavy simmer dishes are batch-produced in the afternoon and only regenerated at service — flattening the peak without a third wok in a 55 m² heritage footprint.

Loaded into Odoo: this daypart model becomes budget lines and Planning demand curves, so post-opening POS actuals are compared to this exact projection.

12-month ramp & break-even

Break-even in month 3 (base case)

The re-opening after the kitchen rebuild ramps from a dinner-led soft launch: month 1 opens at ≈ 37% of steady state and reaches 95%+ by month 8. Fixed monthly costs (labour, rent, contracts, depreciation, overhead) total ≈ RM 98k; contribution per revenue ringgit after food cost and variable utilities is ≈ 66 sen — so the break-even revenue line sits at ≈ RM 148k/month.

An honest number, not a brochure number

Honesty note: these savings are modelled estimates, not guarantees — nameplate loads × diversity factors measured on comparable JR-Tech installations, current TNB tariffs, and Penang F&B wage benchmarks. They are validated at commissioning and then replaced by metered actuals in the Odoo dashboard: the kitchen sub-meter is read on every monthly JR-Tech maintenance visit.

At (RM1,650 + RM2,400) × 60 months, the modelled 5-year total is RM 243,000 — before counting the downtime avoided on a dining room where one lost day is ≈ RM12,350 of revenue.
