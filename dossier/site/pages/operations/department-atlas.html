Constraint 1 — no stock visibility. Every stage from Quote to Resupply queries stock it cannot see: quotes guess availability, pickers search three sites, drivers shuttle transfers, service vans leave without parts, and chemical refills run on memory. One unknown inventory position taxes six departments daily.
Constraint 2 — fire recovery. The burned warehouse means an unplanned re-count, re-layout and insurance reconciliation — painful, but also the single best moment to barcode everything as it goes back on the shelf. Recovery and digitisation should be one project, not two.

Prioritisation matrix — where to point DMAIC first

Impact vs data availability; bubble size = estimated headcount. Top-left (high impact, little data) is where Measure-phase instrumentation pays fastest: warehouse, field service, fleet, chemicals. Fabrication and sales are high impact with partial data — strong second wave.

Reading the matrix

  • Instrument first (high impact, low data): Warehouse & logistics — the fire re-count is a free Measure phase. Field service and chemicals follow: both touch customers weekly and run on paper.
  • Analyse next (high impact, some data): Fabrication (job costing) and sales (quote funnel) — partial records exist in Excel and SQL Account to mine.
  • Control later (lower impact or good data): Finance, HR, marketing — adopt the KPI rhythm once the physical flow is measurable.
  • So what for the Odoo build: this ordering is the module sequence — Inventory + Barcode first, then Field Service and subscriptions-style chemical resupply, then Manufacturing job costing, then CRM/Sales analytics — each module doubling as the department's Measure-phase instrument and OKR data source.
