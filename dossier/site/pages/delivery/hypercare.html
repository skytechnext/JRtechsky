Delivery

Go-live is the start of support, not the end of the project.

The riskiest moment in any ERP programme is the first few weeks of live operation. Our hypercare model surrounds JR-Tech with intensive, fast-response support immediately after each go-live, then deliberately steps that support down as the team becomes self-sufficient — handing over cleanly to a sustainable business-as-usual and application-management arrangement.

The hypercare timeline

Hypercare runs as a step-down: maximum hands-on presence in week one, tapering to scheduled check-ins by week three. Each phase go-live gets its own window, so the team is never carrying a fresh app and a fresh support gap at once.

timeline title Hypercare step-down per go-live Day 0 Go-Live : On-site / on-call all day : Daily stand-up : War-room channel open Week 1 : Same-day fixes : Twice-daily triage : Super-users front-line Week 2 : Response within hours : Daily triage : Ticket trend reviewed Week 3 : Scheduled support : Exit-criteria check : Knowledge base filled Exit : Stabilisation sign-off : Handover to BAU / AMS : Lessons captured
Exit is earned, not timed: hypercare only closes when the ticket burn-down, data-quality and user-confidence criteria are met — see the burn-down chart below. A still-noisy system stays in hypercare.

Ticket volume burn-down

Reading the curve

Ticket volume spikes on day one as the whole team hits the new system at once, then falls steeply as the common questions get answered and super-users absorb the load. The target line is the threshold below which we are comfortable stepping support down.

If actuals track above target into week three, hypercare extends and we investigate root cause — usually a training gap or a missed configuration — rather than simply throwing more support hours at symptoms.

BAU trigger: sustained volume below ~5 tickets/day with no open P1/P2 for five consecutive working days.

Transition to BAU & ongoing AMS

Handover pack

Leave nothing tacit

Configuration workbook, data dictionary, role matrix, runbooks and the knowledge base built during hypercare are handed to JR-Tech's super-users.

Steady state

Self-sufficient first line

Super-users own Tier 1; JR-Tech logs anything beyond into the shared service desk. Most days require no TechNext involvement at all.

AMS option

Retainer for the rest

An optional Application Management Service covers Tier 2–4: upgrades, new requirements, MyInvois rule changes and the Phase 3 AI models — priced as a monthly retainer.

So what for JR-Tech: the model is designed to make TechNext progressively less needed for day-to-day operations, while remaining on call for the genuinely hard problems — aligning incentives toward a system JR-Tech truly owns.
