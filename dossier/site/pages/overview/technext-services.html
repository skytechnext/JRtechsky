1

Organisational Planning — OKRs, KPIs, phases & timelines

Your stated top priority — so it comes first. A plan a 30-person company can actually run: one company objective tree, a KPI dictionary per department, a quarterly rhythm, and gantt-level phases so every initiative has a date and an owner.

What you get

  • Company OKR tree — your vision ("preferred one-stop F&B solutions provider, Go-Green and AI advancements") decomposed into 3–4 annual objectives with measurable key results.
  • Department KPI dictionaries — for sales, fabrication, service, chemicals and accounts: each KPI defined once, with formula, source system and owner, so two managers can never argue about what "on-time delivery" means.
  • Quarterly rhythm — a set cadence: quarterly OKR review, monthly department scorecard, weekly operations stand-up. The meetings exist to move the numbers, not to report them.
  • Phase & timeline gantts — every workstream on this page sequenced with dates, dependencies and owners (see Implementation Roadmap).

A worked example for JR-Tech

O — "Zero-surprise inventory": we always know what we have, where it is, and what it cost.
  • KR1 — Stock record accuracy ≥ 98% on monthly cycle counts (baseline: unknown today).
  • KR2 — Shrinkage and write-offs ≤ 1% of stock value per quarter.
  • KR3 — 100% of equipment and chemical SKUs barcoded and scanned at receipt, transfer and delivery.

Each KR then cascades to department KPIs: KR3 becomes the warehouse team's daily "scans vs. movements" ratio and the drivers' "signed D.O. with scan" rate — numbers Odoo produces automatically, so nobody compiles a report by hand.

2

Business Process Improvement — incl. Six Sigma

Software applied to a messy process just automates the mess. So before and alongside the Odoo build, we run DMAIC-driven process work: map how work actually flows today, measure it, and remove the waste — then let the system lock in the improved process.

Map

Value-stream maps for each department — quote-to-cash, fabrication, chemical delivery, service calls. We have already started: see the Department & Workflow Atlas.

Measure

DPMO baselines on the defects that cost you money — mis-picks, unsigned D.O.s, quote errors, missed service dates. You cannot improve a number nobody records.

Improve & control

A kaizen cadence: small, scheduled improvement cycles owned by your staff, not by consultants — so the gains survive after we leave the room.

First target, agreed on 7 Nov: the warehouse and barcode platform — the "burning platform" where process work and the Odoo build meet. Barcoding is simultaneously a Six Sigma control (it makes the inventory process measurable) and the first visible Odoo win, and it feeds the Zero-surprise-inventory OKR above directly.
3

Odoo ERP Improvement / Implementation

This is the core of this dossier. One Odoo 20 database replaces the patchwork of SQL Account, Excel and WhatsApp: sales, fabrication, rental, field service, chemicals and accounting in the same system, Malaysia-localised (SST, MyInvois e-invoicing to LHDN) and delivered in phases so your team is never asked to swallow everything at once.

What "phased" means

Foundation first (products, contacts, inventory + barcodes, accounting), then quote-to-cash, then field service and rental, then the AI layer. Each phase goes live, stabilises, and pays for the next. Full sequencing in the Implementation Roadmap.

What "improvement" means

Odoo is not install-and-forget. After go-live we keep tuning: new automations, AI document capture, better dashboards — the system improves on the same kaizen cadence as the processes it runs. Architecture in Odoo 20 Architecture.

Future Phase — OEM IoT Enablement

This is where JR-Tech's "AI Technology advancements" vision stops being a slogan. We work with your OEM factories to upgrade equipment electronic chips with WiFi/LAN connectivity. Telemetry from every connected fryer, chiller and dishwasher flows into a custom IoT app — logs and live dashboards — and that app syncs with Odoo, where it drives subscription billing, predictive maintenance, service dispatch and reporting.

Why I can credibly build this

My background is embedded devices and electrical — thousands of floorplans, WiFi and electrical as a specialty, live dashboards as a product (see the owner-to-owner pitch). Why it matters commercially

A connected fleet turns one-off equipment sales into recurring revenue: Equipment-as-a-Service with subscription billing, maintenance triggered by actual machine condition instead of a calendar, and usage data no competitor in Penang can show a hotel GM.
