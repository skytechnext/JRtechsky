Delivery

The ERP is the easy part.
Getting JR-Tech's people to adopt it is the project.

JR-Tech runs on spreadsheets, WhatsApp and the institutional memory of a small, loyal team. Replacing that with Odoo 19 is a behaviour change, not just a software change. This plan uses ADKAR to manage individuals through the transition and Kotter to drive the organisation — backed by a super-user network, a structured comms plan and an honest view of where resistance will come from.

Two lenses: ADKAR for people, Kotter for the organisation

We manage the individual journey and the organisational movement in parallel. ADKAR tells us whether each person is ready; Kotter keeps leadership pushing the whole company in the same direction.

Kotter — the organisational movement

  1. Create urgency. Director frames manual ops as the cap on JR-Tech's "one-stop" growth.
  2. Build the coalition. Director + TechNext PM + line leads own the change visibly.
  3. Form the vision. One backbone, AI-assisted, MyInvois-ready — the maturity leap.
  4. Communicate it. Repeated, role-specific messaging through the comms plan below.
  5. Enable action. Super-users, time to train, removal of "we've always done it this way".
  6. Generate short-term wins. Phase 1 quote-to-cash live in ~8 weeks.
  7. Sustain & build. Each phase compounds credibility into the next.
  8. Anchor the change. Odoo becomes how JR-Tech simply works — old tools decommissioned.

Adoption curve across the rollout

Riding the dip, not pretending it won't happen

Every ERP go-live has a productivity dip as people abandon familiar tools. We plan for it: Phase 1 dips first, then recovers above baseline as the backbone proves itself. Innovators (the super-users) lead, the early majority follow once they see peers succeed, and the laggards — typically long-tenured staff most attached to the old way — are pulled across by reinforcement, not coercion.

Watch the trough: the highest resistance and ticket volume cluster in the first two weeks after each go-live. Hypercare staffing and comms intensity are deliberately weighted there.

Super-user network & training linkage

The multiplier in a 40-person company

We cannot embed a consultant beside every JR-Tech employee — so we build a network of 6–8 super-users, one to two per business line, identified in Phase 1. They are the most respected operators in each team, given early access to the sandbox, deeper training, and a direct line to the TechNext functional consultant.

After go-live they become first-line support: peer help is faster, less intimidating and more credible than a help desk, and it keeps tickets off the consulting team.

Selection rule: pick for influence and willingness, not seniority. A trusted, enthusiastic store clerk converts more peers than a reluctant manager.

How change links to training

The change plan sets the why and when; the training curriculum delivers the how. ADKAR's Knowledge and Ability stages are operationalised entirely through role-based learning paths, and Reinforcement is sustained by hypercare.

  • Super-users are trained one cycle ahead of their teams (train-the-trainer).
  • Each go-live is preceded by just-in-time, role-specific training — not generic decks.
  • Competency sign-off gates a user's access to live transactions.
