Workshop throughput & the constraint (theory of constraints)

The fabrication shop is a five-stage flow. Throughput is set by the slowest stage, not the average — and today that is welding/assembly, sitting at roughly 88% loaded while cutting and design have slack. Subordinating the whole shop to the constraint, then elevating it, is worth more than speeding up any non-bottleneck.

Five focusing steps, applied

  1. Identify — welding/assembly is the constraint (~88% loaded, longest queue of WIP work orders).
  2. Exploit — pre-stage cut & bent parts so welders never wait; protect the bottleneck with a small WIP buffer.
  3. Subordinate — release jobs to the floor at the welding drumbeat, not when sales confirm — flattening the feast/famine cycle.
  4. Elevate — add a second welding bay / shift only once steps 1–3 are exhausted.
  5. Repeat — the constraint then moves (likely to QC/install); re-run.
So-what for the build: Odoo Manufacturing work centres make the constraint visible (capacity %, queue, OEE) instead of inferred. Releasing work to the welding drumbeat is a routing & scheduling configuration, not new software — and it is what converts the ~60% lumpy utilisation into a steady ~80%.

Inventory-policy matrix (ABC / XYZ)

Classifying the seed catalogue by value (ABC) and demand variability (XYZ) sets the right policy per item — instead of one blanket min/max. High-value, lumpy fabrication machines are made-to-order; low-value, steady chemicals and spares run on tight reorder rules.

X — steady demandY — variableZ — sporadic / lumpy
A — high valueCombi ovens, rack/flight dishwashers — forecast & review monthlyChillers, cold rooms — buy-to-order, supplier SLACook & chill, blast chillers — pure MTO, no stock
B — mid valueWork tables, sinks (FAB) — make-to-order on welding drumbeatPrep equipment, glasswashers — low safety stockWalk-in panels, hoods — MTO with raw-material buffer
C — low valueChemicals (min 15 / max 80), warewash detergents — tight reorder rules, auto-POSS raw stock (min 20 / max 100) — buffer for the bottleneckSlow spares — small min, review for de-listing

Reading the matrix against the catalogue

  • A-items (~15% of SKUs, ~70% of value) — high-ticket cooking, refrigeration and warewash equipment. Hold little or nothing; manage by quote and supplier lead time.
  • B-items (~30% of SKUs, ~20% of value) — fabricated tables/sinks and prep gear; MTO sequenced to the welding constraint.
  • C-items (~55% of SKUs, ~10% of value) — the 25 chemicals and 12 accessories already carry min/max reorder rules in the seed data; these run zero-touch with auto-replenishment.
So-what: this is the policy layer behind the ~95→60 inventory-days target — free working capital from A/Z items while never stocking out C-class chemicals.
